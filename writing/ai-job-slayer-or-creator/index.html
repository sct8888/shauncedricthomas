AI: Job slayer, or job creator?

A pragmatic look at automation's impact on the job market, the human skills that remain irreplaceable, and how individuals and businesses shape the outcome.

Shaun Cedric Thomas
Director, Partner Marketing at GetSmarter (a 2U brand)

It's time to dive into a topic that's been buzzing around a lot lately: Artificial Intelligence, or AI as we often call it. The big question on everyone's mind: is AI here to snatch our jobs away, or is it going to give us new, cooler ones?

Let's dive in.

Job slayer?

The fear of job loss due to AI is not unfounded. Automation has the potential to replace repetitive, mundane tasks. Jobs that are rule-based and don't require complex decision-making. Cashiers, data entry operators, telemarketers, and assembly line workers are among those most threatened. Research by McKinsey suggests that by 2030, as many as 800 million jobs could be lost worldwide due to automation.

Additionally, AI is not just about manual labour. It's creeping into areas that require cognitive skills as well. Sophisticated algorithms are now capable of analysing complex legal documents, diagnosing diseases, and writing news articles, encroaching on traditionally 'safe' professions.

Job creator?

On the flip side, history shows us that every technological revolution, while disruptive in the short term, has eventually led to an increase in jobs. The Industrial Revolution, the advent of computers, the internet. All were met with similar concerns, yet they created new industries and job opportunities.

Firstly, while AI may take over some tasks, it cannot replace the human touch. Empathy, leadership, creative thinking, and problem-solving are skills that are uniquely human. The rise of AI could, therefore, lead to an increase in roles that require these 'soft' skills.

Secondly, the AI industry itself will create a multitude of jobs. We will need AI specialists to build and maintain these systems, ethicists to navigate the moral landscape, and trainers to teach AI systems how to perform tasks.

Finally, by automating mundane tasks, AI will free up time for us to engage in higher-value work. This could lead to job transformation rather than job elimination, and a shift towards more fulfilling work.

Navigating the AI landscape

While it's clear that AI will disrupt the job market, it's crucial to remember that we are not passive observers in this scenario. Our actions today will shape the outcome.

As individuals, we can focus on lifelong learning and adaptability. By up-skilling and re-skilling, we can stay relevant in the ever-evolving job market.

As businesses, we can commit to responsible AI practices. This involves creating transition programmes for employees whose jobs are at risk and ensuring that AI is implemented in a way that augments human potential rather than replacing it.

The AI revolution is upon us, and its impact on the job market is undeniable. However, whether it becomes a job slayer or job creator is a narrative we can influence. Let's ensure we steer this narrative towards an inclusive, productive, and human-centred future.
